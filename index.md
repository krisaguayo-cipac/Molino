# Welcome to Molino
"Molino" means mill in Spanish—an ancient technology independently developed by multiple cultures all over the world. In recent centuries, mill technology has transformed, allowing the processing of raw resources at scale, and therefore the consumption of earth's resources at scale.

The unprecedented rate of consumption is preceded by a forethought: The earth is there to be consumed. However, it is not universally recognized that the earth is to be consumed. Contested by alternative ways of knowing, the dominant narrative of the past few centuries has failed us. Large-scale consumption has destabilized earth systems. Today, the Northern Atlantic Ocean current—which pushes cold water south and drives the movement of resources across the ocean ecosystem—is weakening.

Importantly, mills are not inherently destructive. Across centuries, they have been used to make food widely available, addressing the needs of communities. Their impacts are mitigated by their users. At the dawn of their use, mills belonged to their communities—no single individual claimed full ownership. Over thousands of years, this practice has shifted. Today, individuals may own mills and rent out their use to others, controlling the ability to process raw resources and controlling communities' ability to feed themselves.

The application of mills through ownership mirrors the previously mentioned framework: The earth is to be consumed. Both perspectives highlight the mill's essential function—controlling the state of materials across complementary states: raw and processed, ready for use.

This Molino is digital. It does not process grain. It processes what has not been processed—what has been suppressed, left out, or forgotten.
