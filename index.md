<h2 align="center">Welcome to</h2>
<h1 align="center">MOLINO</h1>
Molino is an online repository for open-access research and science-education projects. They are made by Kristopher José Aguayo-Barragán, a scientist and educator focused on integrating disparate knowledge systems and histories.

The name (Molino; Mill in spanish) is inspired by ancient technologies independently developed by cultures all around the world at varying times. Mills transform raw materials into utility. They can serve communities and disperse power, or they may serve individuals and concentrate power.

This mill aims to contribute to the public good by making historical suppressed understanding accessible.

<h1 align="center">Patoli</h1>
<h2 align="center">An Indigenous American Board game</h2>
Patolli has been played on the American continent for well over a thousand years. At its height, the game spanned from the North American Southwest to Central America. The board organizes creation into four domains, each with distinct characteristics, meeting at a cruciform center. This notion of a point of universal symmetry—an axis mundi—appears in human traditions across the globe, from the recession of the ice sheets to today. It is the same intuition that led scientists to propose an expansion from a universal center, a process of perturbation we now call the Big Bang. 

<h3 align="center">This is the kind of integration Molino exists to explore—two frameworks, one ancient and one modern—both describing the same observation: A universe that begins from a center and moves outward.</h3>
